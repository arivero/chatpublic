From here on we are going to introduce into the discussion some uneasy links

  between nuclear and particle mass scales. I am the author of the preprints where

  the first of them appeared (arXiv:nucl-th/0312003 and hep-ph/0405076, 2003-2004),                                                                                                

  and a full numerical audit of them was done in 2026. Please take what follows as

  shared background, so we can discuss the question itself and skip the

  preliminaries.

  THE OBSERVATIONS

  Inputs: PDG 2025 masses, AME2020 atomic masses, 1 u = 0.931494 GeV,

  v = 246.220 GeV from G_F.

  Fission side

  - v = 2 M(132Sn) to +0.19% (246.220 against 245.761 GeV). In mass-number units

    v = 264.3 u, and 264Fm is exactly two 132Sn. Spontaneous fission takes over as

    nuclei approach it: 257Fm lives 100 days, 258Fm 0.37 ms, splitting

    symmetrically into near-132Sn fragments.

  - M_Z + M_H = M(100Sn) + M(132Sn) to +0.19% (216.388 against 215.973 GeV).

    Singly, M_Z is 2.0% below 100Sn and M_H is 1.9% above 132Sn, so the two

    neutral bosons flank the two doubly magic tins.

  - In mass number W = 86.3, Z = 97.9, H = 134.4, top = 185.3. H falls on the

    heavy fission-fragment ridge (A = 133-136, around 134Te with N = 82) that

    every actinide shares. Z falls on the light peak for 238U and 239Pu.

  Fusion side

  - 2 m(D0) = 3729.7 MeV against the 4He nucleus at 3727.4 MeV: the open-charm

    threshold sits 2.3 MeV (0.06%) above the alpha particle. The D0 itself lies

    10.8 MeV (0.6%) below the deuteron.

  Dates

  - The 2003 paper tied W and Z to 100Sn, the Higgs slot to 132Sn and the top to

    208Pb, when the only Higgs candidate was the 115 GeV LEP hint, 6.4% below

    132Sn. The 2012 measurement landed 1.9% from the named nucleus, and the

    pair-sum relation exists only with that measured value.

  - The charm-helium match was noticed in 2026. I have found no earlier mention.

  ALREADY EXAMINED, PLEASE TAKE AS READ

  - Electroweak exchange is far too weak to shape nuclear structure. Contact

    strengths g^2/M^2 give Z exchange at 4e-8 of omega exchange and Higgs exchange

    at 2e-13 of sigma exchange; measured hadronic parity violation confirms the

    1e-7 scale. The bosons mark the closures and the strong force makes them.

  - Exchange depends on momentum transfer (about 250 MeV in a nucleus), so the

    total rest mass of a nucleus enters no propagator.

  - Baryon number conservation forbids any mixing between a B = 0 boson and a

    B = A nucleus.

  - The Higgs does couple coherently to the whole nucleus through the trace

    anomaly (g_hNN = 0.305 m_N/v = 1.2e-3; 0.15 for 132Sn). That fixes how nuclear

    masses respond to v and gives no reason for the matches.

  - Published anthropic windows for the weak scale are 8 to 90 times too wide to

    select these values.

  - The known routes to fixing v/Lambda_QCD (trace-anomaly self-consistency, Higgs

    criticality or asymptotic safety, a scale-invariant origin of v) all leave

    alpha_s as a free input.

  - In the Standard Model the matches pin independent free parameters:

    v/Lambda_QCD, the Higgs self-coupling (m_H = sqrt(2 lambda) v, observed

    lambda = 0.129, close to 1/8) and the charm Yukawa.

  - Unmatched and acknowledged: the top is 11% below 208Pb, and 2 m(B) = 10.56 GeV

    lands on no special nucleus. My 2004 drip-line version is retired.

  TRAPS IN THE STANDARD REPLIES

  - "Magic numbers are derived ab initio." The shell model and its spin-orbit term

    date from 1949, twelve years before the rho and omega were found and a quarter

    century before QCD. In today's nuclear theory "ab initio" means solving the

    many-body problem with controlled approximations, starting from nucleon forces

    fitted to two- and three-body data. In chiral effective field theory only

    pions are explicit, and all shorter-range physics sits in about two dozen

    fitted contact constants plus two for the three-nucleon force. In

    meson-exchange and relativistic mean-field models the sigma is an effective

    field and the omega coupling is fitted. Lattice QCD reaches A <= 4, mostly at

    unphysical pion masses. A derivation of 50, 82 and 126 from QCD does not

    exist yet.

  - "Spin-orbit is understood." The closures at 28, 50, 82 and 126 all need a

    spin-orbit strength an order of magnitude above the Thomas term and of

    opposite sign. Its size comes from fitted scalar and vector strengths, or from

    a fitted contact term.

  - "A real effect would match better than 2%." Nuclear masses come in steps of

    one nucleon, 0.93 GeV, so +-0.5 u is the limit for any single match. The sharp

    statements are the pair sums at 0.19% and the charm threshold at 0.06%.

  - "Look-elsewhere kills it." Trials-factor statistics are built for discovery

    claims with a defined search. These are dated observations, and the fair

    standard is the data available when each was made and what arrived afterwards.

    I make no probability claim and would like to leave that argument aside.

  WHAT I WOULD LIKE FROM YOU

  Treat this as a standing curiosity with an open question: what could relate

  v/Lambda_QCD, lambda and the charm Yukawa to nuclear landmarks, or which further

  check would dissolve the pattern. Useful contributions are mechanisms that

  respect the constraints above (for example states carrying the nucleus's own

  baryon number, relations among parameters, selection arguments with a sharp

  feature), new checks on existing data, and corrections of any factual or

  numerical error you find here.
