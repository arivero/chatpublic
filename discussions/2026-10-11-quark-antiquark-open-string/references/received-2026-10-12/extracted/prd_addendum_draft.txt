    What the warped embedding requires. Pointlike hard scattering with a hadronic Regge slope is ob-
tained in two cases: ν → 0+ , where by the unitarity bound the state approaches a free twist-one field, or
ν < 0, where the state has an elementary component and the hard scale is M∗ . In both cases the degree
of freedom that scatters at large s is a single twist-one field. The anti-diquark–string configuration can
be at most the infrared, composite component of the quark, as in partial compositeness [6]. This is
consistent with the absence of partonic behavior in strongly coupled holographic duals [2].
    Color. Normalizable bulk states of a holographic dual are singlets of the confining gauge group, and
Chan–Paton labels on branes extending along r are global symmetries of that sector. Carrying QCD
color on the Ramond strings therefore requires a confining sector in which SU (3)c is a weakly gauged
global symmetry. In that construction the throat string is not the QCD flux tube, Mhad is replaced by
the infrared string scale of that sector (a few times the lightest Kaluza–Klein mass in Randall–Sundrum
realizations [7]), and the relation 3̄ ∧ 3̄ = 3 of Eq. (56) applies to its constituents rather than to QCD
antiquarks. The literal identification of Eq. (63) with the QCD string is not realized by warping.


16      Lepton–meson realization
The anti-diquark construction of Sec. VIII has a colour-singlet counterpart: the two endpoint labels
form a quark–antiquark pair and the Ramond string supplies spin 21 and lepton number. This section
gives its quantum numbers and Chan–Paton structure, the string factors for the leptonic channels, and
a comparison of both realizations with compositeness limits.

Standard Model fields that carry exactly the SU (3)c × SU (2)L × U (1)Y charges of f (Table 1). When
ψ = χ, Fermi statistics and the antisymmetric Lorentz contraction fix the flavour symmetry to equal

16.2    Chan–Paton realization and selection rules
The lepton–meson state is an oriented open string with labels in N ⊗ N̄ . The charged Ramond state
of Sec. III B, λ± = E12 , E21 , is of this type: the Møller computation of Secs. III–IV is already a
lepton–meson calculation. The quark–diquark state is unoriented, with labels in N̄ ∧ N̄ or N̄ ∨ N̄
from an orientifold. If the labels carry colour only, Λ2 (3̄) = 3. If they carry colour and flavour,
U (9) ⊃ SU (3)c × SU (3)f , then Λ2 (9̄) = (3, 6̄) ⊕ (6̄, 3) and S 2 (9̄) = (3, 3) ⊕ (6̄, 6̄): the anti-diquark that
is antisymmetric in colour and in flavour requires the symmetric projection and comes with colour-sextet
partners.
    The oriented sector has an analogous feature: if the endpoint labels carry colour, 3 ⊗ 3̄ = 1 ⊕ 8 and
the lepton has colour-octet partners, degenerate at tree level. LHC recasts exclude colour-octet leptons
below 1.2–1.55 TeV [10].
    For four Ramond states, the orderings with a nonzero Chan–Paton trace are those in which particle
and antiparticle labels alternate. An explicit enumeration gives the same result for oriented adjoint,
bifundamental, Λ2 and S 2 labels:

e ↔ dū, µ ↔ sū it gives V (s, t). In both realizations the off-diagonal Chan–Paton gauge bosons (e.g.
Esd ) mediate flavour-changing transitions among states that share labels and must be heavy.

six limits, because the leading oscillator effect is dimension eight. In length units the same-stack lepton
string may be as large as 1/Ms = 3.1 × 10−19 m against 1.9 × 10−20 m for a generic dimension-six
composite electron; for quarks the corresponding values are 2.6 × 10−20 m and 5.3 × 10−21 m.

16.5    Lepton–meson with the QCD string
Identifying the endpoint labels with QCD colour fails for three independent reasons: Bhabha scattering
requires Ms > 0.63 TeV, against Mhad ≃ 1 GeV; the colour-octet partners would be degenerate with
the leptons; and a hadron-sized electron gives δae ∼ (me /1 GeV)2 ≈ 3 × 10−7 , against |δae | ≲ 10−12 .
As for quarks (Sec. 15), the labels must belong to a separate confining sector, for which the warped
analysis applies unchanged, since the same factors V appear.
    If the leptonic R is lighter than the pion, Eq. (112) implies the operator (c/Λ2 )(ūR dL )(ēL NR ),
whose pseudoscalar matrix element lifts the helicity suppression of π → eν. The PIENU measurement
of Γ(π → eν)/Γ(π → µν) to 2.4 × 10−3 [13] then gives Λ ≳ 60 TeV for c = 1 (estimate). This test has
no analogue in the quark sector.
