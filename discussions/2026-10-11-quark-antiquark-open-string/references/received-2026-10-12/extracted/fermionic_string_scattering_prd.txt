                     Fermionic Open Strings as Pointlike Scatterers:
             Four-Ramond Amplitudes, Contact Limits, and a Quark Bootstrap
                                                      Alejandro Rivero1

                We study a concrete operational question behind old hadronic bootstrap ideas: how would an
             extended spin-1/2 open string reveal itself in elastic scattering, and can it imitate a point fermion
             over a parametrically large kinematic domain? For a charged Ramond open-string state we derive a
             Møller-like four-fermion amplitude. In a minimal Chan–Paton embedding the complete disk result is
             the gauge-theory amplitude multiplied by a single Veneziano form factor, MRR = V (t, u)Mgauge , so
             the first oscillator correction is O(α′2 E 4 ) rather than the O(E 2 /Λ2 ) term characteristic of generic
             compositeness. We work out the exact angular distribution, the effective dimension-eight contact
             interaction, the crossed-channel Regge poles, and the high-energy fixed-angle suppression. We
             separately analyze the genuinely allowed exchange of a single fermionic mediator in mixed boson–
             fermion channels and its zero-range limit. We then revisit a quark–diquark bootstrap motivated
             by the antisymmetric equivalences 3̄ ⊗ 3̄ ⊃ 3 in color and flavor, old hadronic supersymmetry, and
             modern light-front superconformal constructions. A scalar anti-diquark has precisely the color,
             light-flavor and electric charges of a quark; attaching a fermionic string supplies the required spin,
             while baryon number differs by one unit and must therefore reside in an additional string/junction
             topological sector. Recent baryon-junction evidence makes that assignment phenomenologically
             interesting. However, the common Regge slope of Neveu–Schwarz and Ramond sectors gives a sharp
             obstruction: a hadron-scale bosonic string cannot have a pointlike fermionic partner at multi-TeV
             momentum transfer if both are literally sectors of the same open string. The bootstrap can survive
             only as a dual/operator identification or through a mechanism separating the QCD flux-tube scale
             from the hard Ramond scattering scale. We formulate the resulting experimental tests and distinguish
             them from conventional compositeness searches.
