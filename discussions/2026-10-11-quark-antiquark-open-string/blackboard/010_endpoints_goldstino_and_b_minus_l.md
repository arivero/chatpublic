## What the endpoint labels do and do not specify

In an RNS open-string construction, the NS/R sector and physical-state projection determine the whole state's spacetime statistics. Chan–Paton color/flavor labels by themselves are not four-dimensional endpoint particles with a specified spin. A model with dynamical endpoint particles must supply their boundary fields, action and statistics. Thus the present string proposal leaves these data **underdetermined**, rather than making physical spin-statistics arbitrary.

The selected contact Hamiltonian already specifies two physical spin-1/2 endpoints and a retained spin-1/2 adjoint. The endpoint pair can be in a spin singlet; this does not mean each endpoint has spin zero. Its odd parity comes from three fermions. The user now explicitly opens a separate scalar-endpoint branch for a composite lepton. If the retained carrier is odd, then

| Endpoints | Endpoint fermion parity | Whole parity with an odd carrier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| two fermions | even | odd |
| two scalars | even | odd |
| one scalar, one fermion | odd | even |

The ordinary SQCD mesino, schematically `phi_tilde psi + psi_tilde phi`, is odd **without** an extra odd carrier. It is a distinct candidate whose dictionary can now be investigated explicitly. Supersymmetry acting on a product of scalar endpoint fields normally produces these mixed products; it does not automatically act only by exchanging the glue. In a spin-independent distinguishable-endpoint toy, changing endpoint spins can leave the orbital equation intact. It changes the physical state/operator, allowed interactions and identical-particle constraints, so it is not a completed scalar-endpoint theory.

## The common charge shift

Let $X=B-L$ be a dimensionless number charge, with conventional assignments $X(q)=1/3$, $X(\bar q)=-1/3$, $X(\ell)=-1$, and $X(\bar\ell)=+1$. Consider a candidate color-singlet supercharge $\mathscr Q_+$ of $X$ charge +1 and its conjugate $\mathscr Q_-$ of charge −1. The transition ledger is

| Candidate action | Initial X | Final X | Shift |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| meson → antilepton | 0 | +1 | +1 |
| antidiquark → quark | −2/3 | +1/3 | +1 |
| meson → lepton | 0 | −1 | −1 |
| diquark → antiquark | +2/3 | −1/3 | −1 |

The color representations also agree in each transition: singlet → singlet and 3 → 3 (or anti-3 → anti-3). Electric charges require the corresponding flavor choices. The proposed algebraic assignment is

$$[X,\mathscr Q_\pm]=\pm\mathscr Q_\pm.$$

No convention $\hbar=1$ is used: $X$ is a dimensionless charge count. With a dimensionful generator $\widehat X=\hbar X$, the same statement is $[\widehat X,\mathscr Q_\pm]=\pm\hbar\mathscr Q_\pm$. Conjugate supercharges have opposite charge, so their anticommutator can generate neutral translations. If these are spacetime supercharges, this makes X an **R-type symmetry**, rather than an ordinary flavor charge commuting with supersymmetry. It does not yet specify a representation or dynamics.

Separately, the two +1 transitions require $(\Delta B,\Delta L)=(0,-1)$ and $(+1,0)$. One fixed charge eigenoperator cannot carry both pairs under independently conserved B and L. The common B−L assignment is therefore a real change of symmetry assumption, not a solution retaining both separate charges unchanged. In a retained-carrier interpretation it suggests conjugate carrier branches of X=±1; a self-conjugate Majorana carrier cannot itself carry a nonzero exact continuous X charge. The present number-conserving nonrelativistic carrier can instead be assigned a Dirac number; its relativistic implementation is open.

## A sharper test: the complete Dirac multiplet

The ledger above is consistent at the level of individual transitions. It does not by itself make the six charged mesons a complete linear N=1 representation with conserved physical B−L.

For definiteness use the convention that a left-chiral multiplet's Weyl component has X charge $x-1$ when its scalar has charge $x$ (equivalently the relevant supercharge has charge −1). A charged Dirac lepton is described by two left-handed Weyl fields with X=−1 and +1. Their scalar components would consequently have X=0 and +2. Equivalently, the two scalar **particles with the lepton's electric charge** have X=0 and −2. Ordinary q/qbar mesons both have X=0, so they cannot supply both of these partners under these exact, linear assumptions. Reversing the supercharge convention interchanges which partner is ordinary and which is exotic.

This locates a useful choice for construction: an additional scalar with two units of lepton number, breaking/reassigning this R-type charge, or a nonlinear/emergent realization in which the observed states do not fill those simple multiplets. It is a conditional representation constraint, not a no-go theorem for the user's string proposal. Three charged Dirac leptons have the same spin degrees of freedom as six complex spin-zero fields; equality of counts alone does not resolve this charge/chirality question.

## Pion–muon–Goldstino conjecture

Record explicitly the proposed interpretation

$$\pi^-\longrightarrow\mu^-+\bar G,$$

where the emitted Goldstino state $\bar G$ is tentatively identified with the muon antineutrino in the observed $\pi^-\to\mu^-\bar\nu_\mu$ decay. Its X charge +1 balances the muon's X=−1, and its electric neutrality balances the equal pion/muon electric charges. This is a candidate scalar-partner transition in a broken supersymmetry realization, not a result inferred from particle labels or an attribution to Volkov–Akulov without inspecting their text.

Volkov–Akulov's neutrino-as-Goldstone-fermion hypothesis motivates the question. Nonlinear supersymmetry need not contain a light scalar partner in a linear multiplet. A substantive next test of our additional conjecture would specify the Goldstino coupling and compare the pion's leptonic amplitudes, especially the muon/electron channel ratio and helicity dependence. Those couplings and rates have not been calculated here. No weak-sector completion or three-generation mass fit is presumed.

The user table pairs muon with pi/K, tau with D/D_s, and electron with B/B_c. There are six charged complex pseudoscalar mesons from two up-type and three down-type hadronizing flavors. The top's absence from this ordinary meson list and the large differences between the proposed partner masses need a dynamical account. Preserve this flavor scaffold as a conjecture even where a particular linear realization fails.
