# Scale dependence and historical radiative mass generation

## Running electromagnetic coupling and masses

The manuscript compares **pole/rest masses** with **Thomson-limit** `alpha(0)`. Replacing this with a running coupling at a nonzero momentum while leaving masses fixed is not a consistent RG transformation; it is only diagnostic.

Its own radius is `r_e=alpha*hbar/(m_e*c)`, which implies `hbar*c/r_e≈70 MeV` and the lowest sphere Dirac energy `E*=3hbar*c/(2r_e)≈105 MeV`. Using the spacelike effective QED coupling at those scales, keeping pole masses fixed, worsens the claimed mass agreement. The individual pole mass formulas would prefer different very-low-energy effective couplings rather than one scale.

One-loop QED in a common running scheme has `d alpha/d ln(mu)=(2 alpha²/3pi) Σ_f N_c Q_f²`, and `d ln mbar_l/d ln(mu)=-3alpha/(2pi)` for each charged lepton. Above tau threshold all three lepton ratios have the same electromagnetic anomalous dimension, hence are unchanged at that order even while alpha runs. A fixed `1/alpha(mu)` relation cannot therefore be valid across a range of scales unless other postulate coefficients also run. A compensating `lambda(mu)∝1/alpha(mu)` is possible as an *additional assumption*, not a consequence of H1/H2. Hadronic running and the proton require QCD treatment; a simple QED one-loop calculation is only qualitative.

Koide of pole masses is near 2/3, but using commonly renormalized lepton running masses changes it at roughly the per-mille level in a one-loop matched treatment. Koide is invariant under uniform rescaling of all three masses but not under arbitrary mass-dependent threshold matching. Its geometric cone condition is preserved by RG flow beta_v only if `v.beta_v-2(v.n)(beta_v.n)=0`, with `v_i=sqrt(m_i)` and `n=(1,1,1)/sqrt(3)`.

## Historical literature

The distinction between numerical formulas containing alpha and actual radiative mass generation is essential. Radiative models calculate chirality-changing self energies with loop factors and symmetries preventing arbitrary tree-level Yukawa terms. The manuscript does not.

- Georgi & Glashow, *Attempts to Calculate the Electron Mass* (1973), https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.7.2457
- Barr & Zee, *New Approach to the Electron-Muon Mass Ratio* (1977), https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.15.2652
- Barr & Zee, *Calculating the Electron Mass in Terms of Measured Quantities* (1978), https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevD.17.1854
- A. O. Barut, *The Mass of the Muon* (1978), Phys. Lett. B 73, 310: phenomelogical `m_mu/m_e=3/(2alpha)+1`; not a perturbative loop calculation.
