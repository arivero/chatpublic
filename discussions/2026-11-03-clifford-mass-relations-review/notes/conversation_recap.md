# Chronological recap

Prepared 3 November 2026. User requests are paraphrased; outcomes are summarized from the conversation and the files in this directory. This is not a verbatim transcript.

1. **Transcribe and check.** The user supplied the PDF of A. Kritov, *Clifford Geometry and Mass Relations: From Charged Leptons to the Proton*, version 13 (31 pages), and asked for a LaTeX transcription and a search for mathematical mistakes. The transcription compiles and matches the original word by word apart from ligatures, math-glyph encoding and float placement; it is kept local (see [references](../references/README.md)). 57 operator and Clifford identities were checked numerically with the manuscript's matrices (B.1), and Tables 1 and 3 were reproduced at 40 digits. No error changes a stated result. Findings: the proton comparison omits the uncertainty carried by α; the $m_pc^2$ row is not independent; the uniqueness claim for the return map in Sec. 6.2 needs one more condition; one figure in Sec. 8.2 reads 8.31×10⁻⁷ for 8.30×10⁻⁷; some symbols are reused.

2. **Historical context.** The user asked for the review in the context of Barr–Zee and the history of order-α ideas for e–μ and their extension to μ–τ. The review places the manuscript in the excitation line (Nambu, Barut, Mac Gregor) against the radiative line (Weinberg 1972, Georgi–Glashow 1973, Barr–Zee 1978, later radiative-hierarchy models), translates the muon relation into $m_e/m_\mu = \tfrac23\alpha - \tfrac{34}{63}\alpha^2+\dots$, and shows that the offset 17/14 is smaller than the shifts from changing the scale of α or the mass scheme.

3. **Flexibility.** The user asked which parameters could be changed to fit the target ratios, for leptons and proton. The review enumerates the choice points and alternative formula families (3240 lepton formula pairs, 39 600 proton formulas), counts matches and estimates chance coincidence, and shows that the proton result depends on which α determination is used (Cs and Rb recoil values disagree with the relation at −4.8σ and +2.7σ).

4. **Plots and Koide.** The user asked how constrained the result space is as the inputs move, how Koide's 2/3 changes across the parameter space, and whether some geometry makes Koide exact. Figures were added; Koide crosses 2/3 at α⁻¹ = 137.0455 in the manuscript's formulas and tends to 2.87% above 2/3 as α → 0. A Z₃-circulant square-root mass operator gives Koide identically; the manuscript's 1 + 14 + 1 carrier has no such symmetry.

5. **Discrete choices, running, PDF.** The user asked that quantities the manuscript fixes as integers or simple ratios be scanned only over integers, half-integers or simple ratios, with real quantities varying continuously. The user also asked for a renormalization-group check of whether some energy suits the relations better than the infrared, and for a PDF. On simple-ratio grids every choice is fixed by the data; the nearest neighbour misses by at least 1.8×10⁴σ. Running in an on-shell scheme (dispersive hadronic model) and in MS-bar (with Standard Model running above $M_Z$) finds no better scale: the relations hold only below a few keV, with α at its Thomson value and pole masses. The report is [review/clifford_mass_relations_review.pdf](../review/clifford_mass_relations_review.pdf).

6. **Koide for linear-in-1/α models; commit.** The user remarked that α simplifies out of Koide in Barut-like linear models, while second-order terms complicate it, and asked to commit the discussion here. The expansion and the answer are in [koide_linear_alpha.md](koide_linear_alpha.md): α drops out only at leading order; the electron contributes at order √α, the offsets at order α, so Koide can hold only at isolated α unless the electron scales like the other leptons.

7. **Hierarchy in α.** The user asked whether the manuscript has an α, α² hierarchy or two terms linear in α. The lepton ratios are each two terms, affine in 1/α; the α² series for $m_e/m_\mu$ comes only from inverting them. The proton relation has a third level, $\alpha^2/(3(1-\alpha))$, required by the data at its α² term; its α³ tail is below the uncertainty carried by α.

8. **Draw the geometry.** The user asked for drawings of the spheres and objects that motivate the construction. Figures G1 and G2 were added to the review and its PDF, generated by `scripts/geometry_figures.py`.

9. **Radius, the weights, the split.** The user asked (in Spanish) whether the proposal uses the classical electromagnetic radius of the electron rather than the Compton length, where 0, 1, 17 come from graphically, and whether 1 + 14 + 1 could have been split otherwise. The leptons use $r_e = \alpha\hbar/(m_ec)$; that choice alone supplies the $1/\alpha$, and the Compton length enters only the proton. Figure G3 shows the weights as the traceless part (1 on every word except the identity) plus $\|\mathcal CX\|^2$ (16 on Σ), and compares alternative splits: rotations give 1 + 4 + 3 + 3 + 4 + 1, the U(14) postulate merges the middle 14, and spreading the τ weight over a block of dimension d gives 17/d, which matches only for d = 14.

10. **Comparison with Scott 2023.** The user asked for a discussion of Scott's Physics Forums post (thread 1058414, 21 December 2023), retrieved for this purpose. Scott subtracted Nambu's $\tfrac32\times137$ from the CODATA 2018 ratio, read the digits 1428 as sevenths and found 17/14 within the error (+0.29σ then, +0.39σ with CODATA 2022); he made a weaker remark on the charged pion and did not discuss the τ. Section 2.4 of the review compares the two: the observation is stable across CODATA editions, the pion match carries no information at its precision, and the manuscript's addition is the correlation between the muon offset's numerator and the τ weight 17, with the U(14) postulate selecting the denominator after the target was known.

11. **Companion bundle.** The user supplied `chatpublic_clifford_bundle.zip`, an independent analysis of the same manuscript from a separate conversation, and asked to add its relevant contents to this directory. All of it was relevant and none of it contains the manuscript: notes, two reports with LaTeX, ten PDF figures, two infographics and two scripts. The files were added unchanged, with the bundle's README renamed to `companion_analysis.md`; the directory README compares the two analyses and notes three cautions (inverted sphere sizes in the infographics, a hard-coded output path, a script name mismatch).
