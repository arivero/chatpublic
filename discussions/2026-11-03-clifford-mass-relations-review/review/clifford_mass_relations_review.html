Clifford Geometry and Mass Relations

A. Kritov, Clifford Geometry and Mass Relations: From Charged Leptons to the Proton, v13. Mathematical check of the manuscript, with its lepton relations placed against Barr–Zee and earlier order-α mass ideas.

3 November 2026 · Equation, section and table numbers refer to the manuscript · Inputs as in its Eq. (8.2), CODATA 2022 and PDG 2026

Summary

Mathematics

The derivations in Sections 2–10 and Appendices A–B are internally consistent. The operator identities, flows, channels and colour identities were checked numerically with the matrices of Eq. (B.1), and every number in Tables 1 and 3 reproduces at 40-digit precision. No error changes a stated result. Four points need attention: the proton comparison leaves out the uncertainty carried by α, which is nine times the reference uncertainty used for its z; the mpc² row of Table 3 is not an independent test; the uniqueness claim for the return map in Sec. 6.2 needs one more condition; one figure in Sec. 8.2 should read 8.30×10⁻⁷.

Context

The manuscript belongs to the excitation line of Nambu and Barut, where \(m_\mu/m_e\) is a leading \(1/\alpha\) term with corrections of order one in units of \(m_e\). The radiative line of Weinberg, Georgi–Glashow and Barr–Zee reads the same leading relation from the other side, with \(m_e\) generated at order \(\alpha\) from \(m_\mu\). Read that way, the manuscript fixes the coefficients of \(\alpha\) and \(\alpha^2\) in \(m_e/m_\mu\). The offset 17/14 is smaller than the shift produced by moving α from \(q^2=0\) to the muon scale, and smaller than the difference between pole and running mass ratios, so the relation holds as stated only for α(0) and pole masses.

Flexibility

Section 3 keeps the manuscript's integer and rational choices on simple ratios and lets only α and the energy scale vary. Every discrete choice is then fixed by the data: the nearest simple-ratio neighbour of each misses by at least \(1.8\times10^4\sigma\). On that grid the muon offset 17/14 is the only match, with a chance probability of about \(4\times10^{-3}\) at 1σ. The proton relation has more freedom: its proton-only prescriptions are selected to fit, five screening forms fit equally well once the uncertainty of α is included, and the agreement holds for the CODATA 2022 α but fails by 4.8σ with the Cs recoil value. Running the relations through QED and Standard Model evolution finds no scale better than the infrared: the offsets take the manuscript's values only below a few keV, with α at its Thomson value and pole masses.

Koide

Section 4 follows Koide's ratio through the same parameter space. It is not built into the construction: at the manuscript's point it equals 2/3 to \(10^{-6}\) because the formulas reproduce the measured masses. At fixed weights it drifts with α, crosses 2/3 once at \(\alpha^{-1} = 137.0455\), and tends to 2.87% above 2/3 as α → 0. Exact Koide inside the model needs a τ weight of 17.000115, which is not a simple ratio, and running masses move Q further from 2/3. A square-root mass operator with a cyclic Z₃ symmetry gives Koide identically; the manuscript's 1 + 14 + 1 carrier has no such symmetry.

1Two readings of \(m_\mu/m_e \approx 3/(2\alpha)\)

Numerically \(m_e/m_\mu = 0.0048363\) and \(2\alpha/3 = 0.0048649\), so the leading relation holds to 0.6%. Two lines of work have used it, in opposite directions.

1.1 Excitation line: the heavy leptons as \(1/\alpha\) excitations of the electron

  1. 1952
    Nambu. Empirical particle spectrum organized in units of order \(m_e/\alpha\).
  2. 1978
    Barut, Phys. Lett. B 73, 310. A classical relativistic charged particle has anomalous moment \(g = 4\alpha/3\); quantizing the dressed particle with a generalized Dirac equation gives a second mass \(m_\mu = m_e\,(3/(2\alpha)+1)\).
  3. 1979
    Barut, Phys. Rev. Lett. 42, 1251. Quantized magnetic self-energy \(\tfrac32\alpha^{-1}n^4 m_e\) added level by level: \(m_N = m_e\bigl(1+\tfrac{3}{2\alpha}\sum_{n\le N} n^4\bigr)\). Predicted \(m_\tau = 1786.08\) MeV and a fourth lepton at 10293.7 MeV.
  4. 1992
    BES, Phys. Rev. Lett. 69, 3021. Threshold measurement \(m_\tau = 1776.9^{+0.4}_{-0.5}\pm0.2\) MeV, about 9 MeV below Barut's value.
  5. 2005
    Mac Gregor, Int. J. Mod. Phys. A 20, 719. α-quantized masses and lifetimes across leptons and hadrons.
  6. 2021
    Kritov, Progress in Physics 17, 158. α-quantization of particle masses tied to a cosmological entropy bound.
  7. 2023
    Scott, Physics Forums. Observes numerically that \(m_\mu/m_e - 3/(2\alpha) \simeq 17/14\).
  8. 2026
    Kritov, v13. Keeps Barut's leading weights 1 and 17 and replaces his offsets +1, +1 by +17/14 and −17, derived from a Clifford carrier on \(S^3\) with radius \(r_e\).

1.2 Radiative line: the electron mass as an order-α effect of the muon

  1. 1972
    Weinberg, Phys. Rev. Lett. 29, 388. Masses that vanish at zeroth order because of the representation content can be computed as finite higher-order effects. The example generates the electron mass radiatively from the muon; Raby's review notes a flaw in it, later corrected by Georgi and Glashow.
  2. 1973
    Georgi and Glashow, Phys. Rev. D 7, 2457. Only \(m_\mu\) appears as a counterterm; the electron is massless at zeroth order and acquires a finite mass of order \(\alpha m_\mu\), whose value depends on the masses of unobserved vector bosons.
  3. 1978
    Barr and Zee, Phys. Rev. D 17, 1854. A class of models with \(m_e/m_\mu = N\,\alpha/(\pi\sin^2\theta_W)\), \(N\) a model-dependent number of order one; other corrections are suppressed by making exotic gauge bosons heavy, and the models agree with the neutral-current data of the time. Reproducing the measured ratio needs \(N \approx 0.48\) at \(\sin^2\theta_W = 0.231\).
  4. 1988
    Balakrishna, Phys. Rev. Lett. 60, 1602; Balakrishna, Kagan and Mohapatra, Phys. Lett. B 205, 345. Generations acquire mass one by one at successive orders of perturbation theory.
  5. 1989
    Babu and Ma, Mod. Phys. Lett. A 4, 1975. Review of six basic radiative mechanisms, three at one loop and three at two loops.
  6. 1990
    Babu, Balakrishna and Mohapatra, Phys. Lett. B 237, 221. Supersymmetric Pati–Salam model: τ massive at one loop, μ and e at two loops.
  7. 2008
    Dobrescu and Fox, JHEP 08, 100. All masses from top loops: τ at one loop, μ at three loops, e at four.
  8. 2010
    Graham and Rajendran, Phys. Rev. D 81, 033002. Domino mechanism: masses of successive generations arise at successive orders.
  9. 2021
    Baker, Cox and Volkas, JHEP 05, 174. Minimal one-loop radiative muon mass models, best probed by \((g-2)_\mu\) and the Higgs–muon coupling.

1.3 The step from μ to τ

The two lines separate at the τ. The measured \(m_\mu/m_\tau = 0.0595\) is about 26 times \(\alpha/\pi\), so in the radiative line the μ–τ step needs a different coupling or loop order than the e–μ step; the models above put the τ at one loop and the μ at two or three. In the excitation line both heavy leptons sit on the same \(1/\alpha\) scale and the τ carries an integer weight: Barut's \(1+2^4\), the manuscript's \(1+4^2\), both 17. There α enters the μ–τ ratio only as a correction. From Eq. (7.4),

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{m_\tau}{m_\mu} &= \frac{17(\kappa-1)}{\kappa+17/14}, \qquad \kappa = \frac{3}{2\alpha},\\ &= 17\Bigl[1-\tfrac{31}{21}\alpha+O(\alpha^2)\Bigr] = 16.81795, \end{aligned} \]

against 16.81769(85) from the PDG 2026 τ mass and the CODATA muon mass.

2The manuscript read from the radiative side

2.1 Coefficients of α and α²

Inverting the muon relation of Eq. (8.1) gives the form a Barr–Zee-type calculation would produce:

\[ \frac{m_e}{m_\mu} = \frac{2\alpha/3}{1+\tfrac{17}{21}\alpha} = \frac{2}{3}\,\alpha - \frac{34}{63}\,\alpha^2 + O(\alpha^3). \]

Loop-generated masses usually carry \(\alpha/\pi\) or \(\alpha/4\pi\) times logarithms of heavy masses. In the manuscript the leading coefficient 2/3 comes from the lowest Dirac eigenvalue \(3/(2r)\) at \(r=r_e\) (Eq. 2.6), with no loop factor. In Barr–Zee form the same leading term requires \(N = \tfrac{2\pi}{3}\sin^2\theta_W \approx 0.48\). There \(N\) depends on the model; in the manuscript the corresponding number is fixed by the sphere and by postulates H1–H2, and the second coefficient −34/63 is fixed by the trace redistribution of Sec. 6.

2.2 Which α and which masses

A derivation that produces \(m_e/m_\mu\) at order α has to state which α and which masses it predicts. At the size of the offset 17/14 the choice changes the answer. Leading-order estimates:

Each shift is larger than the offset it is compared with. The manuscript's use of α(0) and on-shell masses (Sec. 9.2) is therefore part of the content of Eq. (8.1), and its agreement at \(9\times10^{-9}\) relative holds in that convention only. Koide's relation has the same issue: it holds for pole masses to \(10^{-5}\) and fails by about 0.2% for running masses at \(M_Z\) (Xing and Zhang 2006). Sumino (2009) built a U(3)×SU(2) family gauge model in which the family-gauge radiative correction cancels the QED correction to Koide's formula. Sec. 11.3 of the manuscript lists self-energy corrections as further research. Given the numbers above, an account of why α(0) and pole masses are the right objects is needed before 17/14 can be read as a structural coefficient. Sec. 3.7 follows the full scale dependence.

2.3 What the data fix

  • Muon. The measured offset is 1.2142839(46); 17/14 lies at +0.39σ. There are 64 reduced fractions \(p/q\) in [1, 2) with \(q\le14\), so the chance that one lies within ±1σ of a point taken at random is about \(64\times9.2\times10^{-6}\approx6\times10^{-4}\). Because 17/14 was found numerically first (Scott 2023, acknowledged in the manuscript), this figure measures the coincidence the construction sets out to explain.
  • Tau. The offset is −17.05(18) with the PDG 2026 mass. It is resolved to ±0.18, so an integer offset falls within 1σ with probability about 0.36. The τ relation tests the weight 17 and the sign of the offset more than its value.
  • Sum rule (11.4). In \(14m_\mu+m_\tau = 93m_e/(2\alpha)\) the muon term carries an uncertainty of \(6\times10^{-5}m_e\) and the τ term \(0.18\,m_e\). At present precision it is the τ test.

3Flexibility: what can be changed to reach the targets

The relations contain no fitted continuous parameter, but each results from a sequence of choices. This section lists those choices, shows which ones the data force, and counts how many alternative formulas the same choices generate. Every number below comes from an enumeration over the alternatives named in the tables, with targets \(m_\mu/m_e = 206.7682827(46)\), \(m_\tau/m_e = 3477.37(18)\) (PDG 2026) and \(m_p/m_e = 1836.152673426(32)\).

3.1 Lepton choice points

The knobs of Eq. (11.1), the radius factor \(\lambda\), the structural weight \(g\) and the calibration \(\eta\), are set to 1 by H1–H2, and the other choice points are integers or simple ratios: 3/2, 17 and 14 for the leptons, 9, 1 and 1/3 for the proton. The scans below keep them on simple ratios, meaning integers, half-integers and fractions with denominator up to 4, with dimensions as integers. Only α and, in Sec. 3.7, the renormalization scale vary continuously.

3.2 Counting lepton alternatives

  • Muon offset. Offsets of the form \(\eta N/D\), with \(N, D\) from the integers the carrier supplies {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 14, 15, 16, 17, 31} and \(\eta\in\{1/2, 1, 2\}\), give 137 distinct values in (0, 3]. Only 17/14 lies within 2σ of the data. A random offset in (0, 3] would be matched within 1σ with probability \(4.2\times10^{-4}\), within 2σ with \(8.4\times10^{-4}\).
  • Rational grid. With \(g\) and η on fractions with denominator ≤ 4 up to 3, and \(d\) from 1 to 20, the offsets \(\eta(1+16g)/d\) take 1443 distinct values in (0, 3]. Only 17/14 lies within 2σ. A random offset would be matched within 1σ with probability \(4.4\times10^{-3}\), ten times the figure for the smaller integer set above, since the probability grows with the grid.
  • Joint family. 6 unit energies × 3 dimensions of \(S\) × 3 trace normalizations × 4 redistribution spaces × 3 calibrations × 5 offset rules give 3240 formula pairs. One, the manuscript's, fits both leptons within 2σ. Over 200 000 random targets (\(m_\mu/m_e\) uniform in [150, 300], \(m_\tau/m_e\) in [2000, 5000], same uncertainties) the family matched the muon 5 times and both leptons never.
  • Reading. Inside this grammar the lepton formulas are rigid: the leading terms are forced at the percent level and the muon offset carries a look-elsewhere probability near \(10^{-3}\). The grammar was written after the values were known, and its size is the input these numbers depend on most: doubling the set of rules or integers roughly doubles the probabilities.

3.3 Proton choice points

  • Counting. \(W\) × reference length × \(m\) × sign × \(f\) gives 39 600 formulas. Five fit within 2σ; all have \(W = 9\), the Compton subtraction and −1/3, and differ only in \(f\). A random target in [1830, 1842] would be matched within 1σ with probability \(3.3\times10^{-5}\), or \(4.2\times10^{-6}\) with the reference-only uncertainty that Table 3 uses.
  • Leading constant. The chance that \(27/(2\alpha)-\rho_p\) lies within its observed distance \(1.79\times10^{-5}\) of some fraction with denominator ≤ 6 is \(4.3\times10^{-4}\), comparable to the muon offset.
  • Screening term. The α² term is 63 times the model uncertainty, so some such term is required. Its form is not determined: the five fitting forms differ from the manuscript's by \(1.3\)–\(2.6\times10^{-7}\) in \(\rho_p\), below the \(2.8\times10^{-7}\) carried by α. Separating the closest of them at 3σ would need \(u(\alpha^{-1})\approx2\times10^{-9}\), ten times below the present value.

3.4 Dependence on the α input

At their precision the lepton relations do not depend on which α is used. The proton relation does. It passes with the CODATA 2022 adjustment and with the electron \(g-2\) value, and fails at −4.8σ with the Cs value and +2.7σ with the Rb value, counting the α uncertainty. With the CODATA 2018 α the same formula would have shown \(z = -39\) in the units of Table 3.

3.5 Fitted and predicted

  • Forced by the data at the percent level: the unit energy \(\tfrac32\alpha^{-1}\), the τ weight 17, the proton weight 9.
  • Selected at high precision among the enumerated alternatives: the redistribution space 14 and \(\eta = 1\) (muon, ppb level); the Compton subtraction, the term 1/3 and the α² term (proton).
  • Tested after selection: the τ offset −17, to ±0.18, which excludes the neighbouring rule at 7σ; the proton α² coefficient, to ±1.6%.
  • Scale: fixed to the infrared; Sec. 3.7 finds no other scale where the relations hold.
  • Not tested: the form of the screening term at present α precision, and the use of α(0) with pole masses, since any other convention moves the results by more than the offsets.

Figure 1 moves one choice at a time across its grid of simple ratios, with the others held at the manuscript's values, and plots the resulting pull. For the leptons the pull is the larger of the muon and τ pulls; for the proton it includes the uncertainty carried by α. Each manuscript value sits below 1σ. The nearest neighbour misses by at least \(1.8\times10^4\sigma\) (redistribution dimension 15), and in four of the eight panels by more than \(10^6\sigma\). On these grids there is nothing to tune: every discrete choice is fixed by the data, and only the split of the leading 3/2 between the Dirac level and the radius, \(c/\lambda = 3/2\), is degenerate.

3.7 Running: is there a better scale than the infrared?

The manuscript uses α at \(q^2 = 0\) and pole masses. To test whether another energy suits the relations better, each relation was written as the offset it needs at a renormalization scale μ,

\[ o_\mu = \frac{m_\mu}{m_e}-\frac{3}{2\alpha(\mu)},\qquad o_\tau = \frac{m_\tau}{m_e}-\frac{51}{2\alpha(\mu)},\qquad o_p = \frac{m_p}{m_e}-\frac{27}{2\alpha(\mu)}+\frac{27}{2}, \]

and evaluated in two schemes.

  • On-shell. Pole masses and the effective coupling \(\alpha_{\rm eff}(\mu) = \alpha/(1-\Delta\alpha(-\mu^2))\): exact one-loop leptonic vacuum polarization, and a dispersive hadronic part built from narrow ρ, ω, φ, J/ψ, ψ(2S) and Υ resonances with their measured \(\Gamma_{ee}\) plus perturbative continua, calibrated to \(\Delta\alpha_{\rm had}^{(5)}(M_Z^2) = 0.02783\) (PDG 2024). The band shows the spread for 0.02761 to 0.02795 and for a light-quark continuum \(R\) from 2.0 to 2.3.
  • \(\overline{\rm MS}\). Running masses at a common scale and \(\hat\alpha(\mu)\): leptonic running at two loops with decoupling, the hadronic part rescaled to \(\hat\alpha^{(5)}(M_Z)^{-1} = 127.930\), and above \(M_Z\) one-loop Standard Model running of \(\alpha_{\rm em}\) with the lepton mass ratios held fixed, since the gauge terms in their running are flavour-universal.
  • On-shell. The offsets equal the manuscript's values only as μ → 0 and grow monotonically with the scale: muon 1.214, 2.69, 5.44, 13.4; τ −17.05, +8.1, +54.8, +190; proton −0.333, +13.0, +37.7, +109, at μ → 0, \(m_\mu\), \(m_\tau\) and \(M_Z\). The relations stay within 1σ only below μ ≈ 6 keV (muon), 300 keV (τ) and 0.5 keV (proton), all below the electron mass.
  • Offset rule. The manuscript ties the two lepton offsets by \(o_\tau/o_\mu = -d = -14\). The ratio is −14.01 as μ → 0, −11.9 at 1 MeV, −2.9 at 10 MeV and +2.9 at 100 MeV.
  • \(\overline{\rm MS}\). The QED mass anomalous dimension is the same for e, μ and τ, so ratios at a common scale stop running above the τ: \(\bar m_\mu/\bar m_e = 210.66\), \(\bar m_\tau/\bar m_e = 3578.2\). With \(\hat\alpha(\mu)\) still running, the muon offset goes from 6.8 at \(m_\mu\) through 10.0 and 18.8 at \(m_\tau\) and \(M_Z\) to 47 at \(10^{16}\) GeV, and the τ offset from 167 to 797. Neither approaches 17/14 or −17: the pole-to-\(\overline{\rm MS}\) conversion alone raises \(m_\mu/m_e\) by 1.9%.
  • Reading. No scale suits the relations better than the infrared. A continuous scale can place one offset on any chosen value (the on-shell muon offset passes 3/2 at 2.6 MeV, 2 at 14 MeV and 3 at 200 MeV), which is the flexibility counted earlier in this section. Two relations take simple values at a common scale only as μ → 0, with α at its Thomson value and pole masses. Koide behaves the same way: with \(\overline{\rm MS}\) masses it is off by \(+1.74\times10^{-3}\) at every scale above \(m_\tau\), consistent with Xing and Zhang.

4Koide's ratio across the parameter space

With \(Q = (m_e+m_\mu+m_\tau)/(\sqrt{m_e}+\sqrt{m_\mu}+\sqrt{m_\tau})^2\), the manuscript evaluates \(Q/(2/3)-1\) as a diagnostic (Eq. 8.3). Its range is \(1/3\le Q\le1\); \(Q = 1\) would need two vanishing masses, so the question of interest is where \(Q/(2/3) = 1\).

4.1 At the manuscript's point

The relations give \(Q/(2/3)-1 = -1.02\times10^{-6}\); the measured masses give \((-3.3\pm7.6)\times10^{-6}\), the uncertainty set by the τ mass. The model's value comes from its fit to the masses. Its \(m_\tau = 1776.957\) MeV and the τ mass that makes Koide exact with the measured e and μ, 1776.969 MeV, differ by 0.012 MeV, 0.13σ of the PDG mass. A τ mass measured to about 0.004 MeV would separate the two at 3σ.

Exact Koide would need a τ weight of 17.000115, which is not a simple ratio: the weight is \(1+(\mathrm{tr}\,\Sigma^2)^2\), an integer.

4.3 Koide's ratio as a function of α

At fixed weights the ratio drifts with α and crosses 2/3 once, at \(\alpha^{-1} = 137.0455\). As α → 0 it tends to \(18/(1+\sqrt{17})^2 = 0.6858\), 2.87% above 2/3. In this model Koide's value therefore depends on the electron term at finite α. With every mass linear in \(1/\alpha\) and the electron as the unit, \(Q = 2/3\) cannot hold for all α: \(\mathrm{Tr}\,M - \tfrac23(\mathrm{Tr}\sqrt M)^2\) contains the term \(-\tfrac43(1+\sqrt G)\sqrt\kappa\), \(G\) the τ weight, which no choice of constant offsets removes.

Figure 6 gives the τ weight \(G\) that would make Koide exact at each α, keeping the manuscript's offset rule (\(+G/14\), \(-G\)). It runs from 17.58 at \(\alpha^{-1} = 100\) through 17.0001 at the measured α to \(7+4\sqrt3 = 13.93\) as α → 0, the value for which \(\sqrt{m_\tau/m_\mu} = 2+\sqrt3\). The limit is irrational, so no integer weight \(1+n^2\) reaches it. The weight 17 meets the curve only at \(\alpha^{-1} = 137.0455\).

4.4 A geometry with Koide built in

Koide's ratio is a statement about \(\sqrt M\). \(Q = 2/3\) holds exactly when the vector \((\sqrt{m_e},\sqrt{m_\mu},\sqrt{m_\tau})\) makes 45° with \((1,1,1)\) (Foot 1994), that is, when the traceless part of \(\sqrt M\) has the same Hilbert–Schmidt norm as its scalar part. A Z₃-circulant square root,

\[ \sqrt M = \mu\Bigl[I + \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\bigl(e^{i\delta}T + e^{-i\delta}T^\dagger\bigr)\Bigr], \qquad T\ \text{the cyclic shift of the three profiles}, \]

has eigenvalues \(\mu\bigl(1+\sqrt2\cos(\delta+2\pi k/3)\bigr)\) and gives \(Q = 2/3\) for every μ and δ; the masses then fix δ. This parametrization is standard in the Koide literature. The manuscript's carrier has neither ingredient. Its balance between scalar and nonscalar parts is imposed on \(M\), through the graph norm on \(\mathcal W\), and its profiles sit in blocks of dimensions 1, 14, 1 with no symmetry that exchanges them. A carrier with Koide exact would need three sectors permuted by a cyclic symmetry and the 45° condition on the square-root operator. The angle is 44.99997° for the manuscript's masses and 44.99991(22)° for the measured ones.

5Findings of the mathematical check

  1. ModerateThe proton comparison omits the uncertainty carried by α

    Sec. 10.5, Table 3, abstract, Sec. 11.3

    Since \(\partial\rho_p/\partial\alpha^{-1} = 27/2\), the uncertainty \(u(\alpha^{-1}) = 2.1\times10^{-8}\) gives \(u(\rho_p) = 2.8\times10^{-7}\), which is 8.9 times the CODATA \(u(m_p/m_e) = 3.2\times10^{-8}\) used for z. Against the model uncertainty the residual \(1.11\times10^{-8}\) is 0.04σ. Equivalently, Eq. (10.12) solved for α gives \(\alpha^{-1} = 137.0359991762(24)\), consistent with CODATA. The statement in Sec. 8.2 that input uncertainties are negligible holds for the leptons only. The sentence "at the central input values" is accurate; the test is limited by α. A model-uncertainty column in Table 3, or the implied value of \(\alpha^{-1}\), would state this. Sec. 3.4 shows how the comparison changes with the α input.

  2. MinorThe \(m_pc^2\) row is not an independent test

    Table 3

  3. MinorThe return map is fixed only on active inputs

    Sec. 6.2, Eqs. (6.5)–(6.7)

The claim "these conditions determine the return map" needs \(\mathscr J(P_e)=0\) or the trace condition on all inputs; the latter also gives \(\mathrm{Tr}\,\mathscr L(Z) = 0\) in Eq.
