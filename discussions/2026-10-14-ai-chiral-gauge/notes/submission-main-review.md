## What the main result actually claims

Theorem `thm:measure`, lines 1026–1051, asserts a smooth gauge-covariant, exponentially local current for overlap fermions in SU(5) `10 + 5bar`, on sufficiently small admissible plaquettes, satisfying both the anomaly divergence equation and full closed-loop integrability. The admissibility threshold and positive locality rate are independent of volume, component and derivative order; derivative-dependent constants remain allowed. The measure is determined only up to one phase per connected component. The paper then restricts the result to the four Standard Model gauge groups.

It does **not** claim a practical dynamical simulation or an established interacting continuum limit. Conclusions, lines 3512–3514, explicitly leave practical implementation, optimal admissibility bounds, relative component phases, reflection positivity compatible with admissibility, and the interacting continuum theory open.

## Concrete algorithm and the difficult mathematical work

1. **One reconstructed connection, retained throughout refinement.** Section `sec:construction`, lines 1252–1357, reconstructs a smooth SU(5) connection from the original admissible links. The fine links are samples of that one connection; they are not obtained by repeatedly reconstructing the previous fine field. The reconstructed sample at spacing one need not equal the original links, so `U^(0)=U` is essential. The first comparison is separately controlled.
2. **Geometric reconstruction is explicit but substantial.** Appendix `app:g:geometry`, particularly lines 3749–3940, constructs weighted short-transport matrices `K`, selects their five eigenvalues near one by a contour projector, orthonormalizes local frames, synchronizes their determinant phases, and removes the trace from the projected connection. This accommodates nontrivial bundles without requiring a global color frame. It bounds curvature by the plaquette deviation; original-link derivatives need not retain that small factor. This is much more than taking matrix roots of individual links.
3. **Embedding and frame derivatives matter.** Lines 1304–1337 and 1359–1414 define inherited frames by parent-to-child transport, 81 centered children, and the isometric copying factor `1/9`. `dI=0` holds in those component coordinates. It does not permit discarding derivatives of the connection, initial frames, or inherited transports. Lines 1566–1575 state this explicitly.
4. **Finite identities are exact.** Lemma `lem:transgression`, lines 1077–1236, defines `b = i integral Tr P[partial_s P, delta P] ds`; with `s=0` fine and `s=1` embedded coarse, `db=Omega_coarse-Omega_fine` and `exp(i integral_loop b)=T_coarse/T_fine`. Lines 1643–1675 telescope these identities. A zero of the comparison operator would invalidate the projector construction; a gap at endpoints alone is insufficient.
5. **The free comparison has an analytic argument.** Appendix `app:s:spectral`, lines 4473–4723, reduces the 324-dimensional zero-mode problem to four dimensions and proves a sine-sign inequality, with separate corner cases. At `p=0,s=1/2`, the diagonal *decomposition* is singular but the full comparison is not; the embedded coarse correction restores the zero-momentum block. Compactness then supplies some positive gap, without specifying a numerical optimal bound. The earlier numerical free-gap check tests this ingredient but cannot replace its universal argument.
6. **The crucial cancellation is stronger than terms merely tending to zero.** Section `sec:locality`, lines 1804–1966, derives equality of ordered color traces in `R` and `R*` through four Lie-algebra factors from cubic anomaly cancellation. Spin conjugation gives the opposite sign. A current insertion already supplies one factor, so four background derivatives are needed before a nonzero Taylor remainder can survive. Each radial background insertion costs `h^2`: the per-site current bound is `h^8`, and the `h^-4` site count leaves `h^4`. Curvature has two prescribed insertions, hence `h^6` per site and `h^2` after summation. The complete multiplet must be summed *before* absolute values. Equation `eq:scale-bounds`, lines 2095–2113, makes these uniform claims precise.
7. **Two kinds of differentiation must not be confused.** Appendix `app:a:locality`, lines 5668–5775, scales an auxiliary local radial potential with parameter `u`, while holding the actual induced positive-link generators fixed only in `u`. Their dependence on the original links remains differentiated. Reverse-link insertions must still vary consistently. Recomputing variations from the newly scaled background would test a different object. These details are pivotal to claims about original-link derivatives and locality.
8. **Local cancellation alone does not fix global phase.** Section `sec:global`, lines 2450–2538 and 2687–2825, constructs the history bundle over `T^4 x S^1`, splits off two global color sections, and chooses an auxiliary SU(3) connection. The multiplet restricts to `2(3)+2(3bar)+3(1)`. Color conjugation combined with spin conjugation gives an antiunitary of square `-1`; paired return eigenvalues fix the auxiliary covariant determinant phase to exactly `+1`, including its sign. A fixed cylinder and color-transport correction connect this to the desired ordinary projector phase. Bounds `O(h^2)` and `O(h^4)` remove those two corrections. The cylinder is not a contraction of the original loop, and constants may depend on the fixed loop. Appendix `app:d:cylinder-traces`, lines 6190–6306, supplies the corresponding estimates.

## What was established before, and what is claimed new

The paper itself credits Lüscher for the ordered-trace/charge-conjugation cancellation (line 1843), earlier work for continuum interpolation (1256–1258), local bundle reconstruction (3554–3557), and approximate-cocycle projection methods (3760). It is therefore inaccurate to present refinement, telescoping, or diminishing residuals as new inventions.

The proposed advance is to assemble these ingredients into a nonperturbative prescription that simultaneously keeps a gapped projector comparison, summable original-link derivative bounds, exponential locality, and global loop consistency for the stated non-Abelian chiral representations. Lines 143–145 explain why simply shrinking a non-Abelian gauge potential can leave the admissible domain: derivative and commutator terms scale differently. Refinement samples a fixed smooth connection instead. The legitimacy of the universal estimates and their hypotheses is the point requiring independent scrutiny.

## Consequences for our running computation

The upload supplies detailed main-source equations but no missing author implementation. Our homogeneous, noncommuting four-dimensional background calculation can test representation cancellation and recovery of vectorlike/Dirac overlap determinants. It does not implement the Appendix A reconstruction, the full inherited-frame transgression current, all locality derivatives, or arbitrary noncontractible loops. Success in that campaign cannot establish the theorem.

Three different limits must remain distinct: auxiliary refinement `h -> 0` at fixed original lattice; removal of the original physical cutoff `a -> 0`; and increasing the number of evaluated momentum points. Appendix `phys:section`, lines 6849–6908, expressly separates them. Its stronger `O(a^6)` exact-current estimate (7160–7246) is for specified smooth classical background families, not a quantum continuum-limit proof.

Verification section `sec:verification`, lines 3493–3507, reports sixteen Lean modules and foundational-axiom checks, but expressly says they establish the formal statements under their recorded assumptions; correspondence to the companion was separately reviewed by AI agents. The uploaded main source does not let us rerun those missing modules. The acknowledgments (3519) describe an Astra-led proof developed from Craig's strategy, author rewrites, and Astra-executed checks. These are the author's provenance statements, not independent validation by this review.
