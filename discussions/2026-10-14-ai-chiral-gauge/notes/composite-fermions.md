# Light composite fermions and the retained adjoint

A composite fermion can be light and appear pointlike over a finite momentum range. Those are two separate requirements. A small relativistic pole mass needs a protection mechanism; an unresolved internal structure needs a sufficiently small current radius or another demonstrated suppression of the relevant form factors.

For a normalized elastic charge form factor at physical spacelike momentum transfer Q,

F(Q) = 1 - Q² r_E²/(6 ℏ²) + higher powers.

Thus scattering cannot resolve that radius when Q r_E/ℏ is small. This is a statement about a specified probe and momentum range, not a proof that every interaction sees an elementary particle. A symmetry can protect a mass m_Ψ c_light² far below the compositeness energy without automatically suppressing the charge radius. The dimensionless hierarchy m_Ψ c_light r_E/ℏ can therefore be small, but must be explained dynamically.

## The three-fermion color channels

Let q transform as 3, qbar as bar3, and the retained fermion χ as the adjoint 8 of SU(3). Three fermionic constituents have odd fermion parity; appropriate spin couplings can produce a spin-one-half state. Their color representation depends on the endpoint orientations:

- q qbar χ can be a singlet, because 3 × bar3 contains 8 and 8 × 8 contains 1. A schematic spinor interpolator is H = (qbar T^a q) χ^a, with a Lorentz-scalar endpoint bilinear. This matches the colorless fermionic branch.
- q q χ cannot be a singlet: its SU(3) center charge is that of two fundamentals, since the adjoint is center-neutral. It does contain an anti-triplet channel. This is a representation statement, not a prohibition on colored composite operators.
- qbar qbar χ contains a triplet, as required for the proposed quark branch. Write a scalar antidiquark D^i proportional to ε^{ijk} qbar_j qbar_k, with flavor and spin contractions satisfying Fermi statistics. Then Ψ^i = (T^a)^i_j D^j χ^a transforms as 3.

A colored quark interpolator is gauge-covariant rather than a gauge-invariant isolated asymptotic state in a confining theory. The earlier contact Hamiltonian uses global color, so promoting its channels to a gauge theory requires that additional distinction. The singlet obstruction above is specific to SU(3); for example, SU(2)'s pseudoreal fundamental has different tensor products.

## The adjoint is an active binding constituent

In the selected model χ remains in the state and binds both endpoints through the two regulated endpoint–adjoint contacts. There is no direct endpoint force. It must not be replaced by an inert spectator attached to a preformed meson.

Calling χ a binder is consistent with that Hamiltonian. Calling it a gauge-force carrier would assert something additional: ordinary gauge connections are bosonic, while an adjoint fermion such as a gluino is a different degree of freedom. Fermion parity prevents a conventional single-fermion classical expectation value; it does not imply a contact propagator or pointlike scattering. A relativistic completion must derive the chosen contact interactions and their range.

The existing bound E_3 ≤ 2 ε_2 < ε_2 establishes stability below the specified breakup threshold. It does not establish a small relativistic mass. The nonrelativistic calculation has subtracted constituent rest energies; tuning its binding energy to cancel approximately (2m + M)c_light² would generally leave its controlled regime.

## The next bridge

A candidate relativistic completion needs an unbroken symmetry forbidding a mass for the desired composite, or a controlled small breaking of that symmetry. Anomaly matching can constrain possible light composite spectra under assumptions about the infrared phase; it does not by itself prove that confinement produces the proposed state. Supersymmetric confining theories provide controlled examples of massless composite fermionic degrees of freedom, but their operators and scalar constituents cannot simply be relabeled as this three-fermion construction.

For the retained-adjoint model, the useful next connection to experiment is its full current matrix element and excitation spectrum. The already positive radius and its anharmonic correction make the pending finite-Q form factor meaningful. Establishing binding, a protected mass, and unresolved scattering are distinct milestones; success at the first does not substitute for the other two.
